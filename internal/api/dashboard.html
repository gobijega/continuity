What you are looking at

The idea

Conventional network selection picks whichever link is fastest right now — lowest latency, highest bandwidth. Continuity adds a Mission Context Engine that also understands why communications matter at this moment, and re-weights the decision accordingly. The result: the same network conditions can produce a different, better decision as the mission changes.

How a decision is made

Every bearer gets a conventional network score, then a mission score (the same live metrics re-weighted by mission policy, plus a structural-resilience term), multiplied by a mission suitability factor for that bearer type. The highest effective score wins, subject to anti-flap hysteresis so the system never oscillates on noise.

Glossary

Mission profile
The standing objective (Routine, Mission Critical, Emergency, Contested) that sets how bearers are weighed.
Mission state
Operational pressure (Normal → Critical), which sharpens the weighting toward reliability and resilience.
Effective score
Network score × mission suitability — the value the selector actually ranks on.
Mission influence
How far mission policy moved the decision away from a purely network-based one.
Hysteresis
Anti-flap logic: a bearer must be clearly and persistently better before Continuity switches.

Status of this demonstrator

This console runs on simulated bearers, simulated mission profiles and representative scenarios. It is autonomous, policy-driven and explainable — it is not AI/ML. Nothing here is field-proven, certified, or accredited; real-hardware integration and operational validation are future work.
